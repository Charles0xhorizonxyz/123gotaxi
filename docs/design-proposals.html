Four ways to look like a taxi app with a sense of humour

Each direction keeps the same two-tap flow and the same screens. Only the logo, colours, type and jokes change. Pick one, or mix them, and the working site gets restyled to match.

Passenger: two taps

  1. 1Paste a map link, or pick a search suggestionThe QR appears on its own. No "generate" step.
  2. 2Turn the phone aroundSave and download are there, but never in the way.

Driver: two taps

  1. 1Scan with the cameraOpens the gogogo.rodeo page, nothing to install.
  2. 2Tap their navigation appThe route starts. With "remember", next time it's zero taps.

Rules every direction keeps

  • Jokes live in words and the logo, never in the buttons. A button always says what it does.
  • The QR is always black on white, whatever the theme. Cameras need contrast.
  • Buttons are thumb-sized and readable at arm's length in a dark car.
  • The driver screen reads in two seconds: where to, then which app.

Direction 1

Sarı Rodeo

Recommended

Istanbul's yellow taxi, roped in. Taxi yellow, asphalt black and the checker strip from the cab door. The logo is a cowboy hat with a checker band. Drivers recognise "taxi thing" before they read a word.

Palette

Taxi yellow Asphalt Seat cover Checker

Type

CIHANGIR Archivo Black for names, Archivo for everything else. Wide, heavy, readable from the driver's seat.

Voice

  • TaglinePaste it. Show it. Yeehaw.
  • Saved placesYour stable
  • Short-link errorThat short link bucked us off. Open it and copy the long one.
  • Taxi cardScan me, driver. I know where I'm going.
Works because
  • Reads as "taxi" instantly in Istanbul
  • Highest contrast of the four
  • Print card looks great on yellow stock
Watch out
  • Yellow is loud; dark mode becomes black with yellow details

Direction 2

Sheriff of the Bosphorus

Full western, at night. Denim navy, a brass sheriff's star with a map-pin hole and wood-type headings. The passenger "deputizes" the driver. It's the most rodeo of the four, and the slowest-feeling.

Palette

Denim Brass Bone Saddle

Type

Cihangir Rye wood type for names only, Work Sans for buttons and text.

Voice

  • TaglineDeputize your driver.
  • Saved placesKnown hideouts
  • Short-link errorThis link is wearing a disguise. Open it and copy the real one.
  • Taxi cardWANTED: one ride to Cihangir. Reward: a tip.
Works because
  • Strongest, most memorable joke
  • Calm at night in a dark cab
Watch out
  • Western type gets hard to read on long Turkish names
  • Feels "themed", less "tool"

Direction 3

Nazar Express

The evil-eye bead every Istanbul taxi already has hanging from its mirror, turned into a map pin that occasionally winks. Friendly, rounded and local. The joke: your route is protected from wrong turns.

Palette

Nazar blue Iris Pupil Glass

Type

Cihangir Bricolage Grotesque throughout: warm, a little quirky, handles ğ ş İ well.

Voice

  • TaglineProtected from wrong turns since today.
  • Saved placesYour lucky spots
  • Short-link errorThis short link has the evil eye. Open it and copy the long one.
  • Taxi cardScan for a blessed route.
Works because
  • Deeply local, made-in-Istanbul feel
  • Pin logo works tiny as an app icon
Watch out
  • Drops the rodeo theme; the name and look drift apart
  • Blue is also Google's and Yandex's colour

Direction 4

One Lasso

Almost no decoration. The logo is a single rope stroke that loops into a map pin. Green means go, and the destination is huge. The humour is only in the slogan and the occasional line of copy. It's the fastest-feeling and the easiest to trust.

Palette

Paper Rope Go green Kerb

Type

Cihangir Instrument Sans, plus a monospace face for coordinates.

Voice

  • TaglineTwo taps. No chit-chat.
  • Saved placesSaved
  • Short-link errorShort links hide the location. Open it and copy the long link.
  • Taxi cardScan to navigate.
Works because
  • Feels fastest and most trustworthy
  • Easiest to keep consistent
Watch out
  • Least funny; could be any app
  • Easy to forget

My pick

Sarı Rodeo, with the nazar bead as a hidden extra

It's the only direction that says "taxi" before anyone reads a word, and its contrast is the best for a driver glancing at a phone at night. The jokes stay in the copy, so the buttons stay obvious. One idea from Direction 3 is worth borrowing: a tiny nazar bead in the footer that winks once.
